# The hail picker is the only place the console names a person

A hail is issued from the destination loop, not from a directory. Opening the picker on a loop card lists the **roles permitted to hear that loop**, each expanding to **whoever currently occupies it** — so the same control hails a role or one named person, at two levels of the same list. It opens filtered to targets that can actually be hailed; a toggle reveals everyone else, greyed out and each saying why in a few words.

Anchoring on the loop is what keeps this small. The operator console is two views of one *loop* list ([ADR-0032](./0032-the-console-is-two-views-of-one-loop-list.md)) and has never contained a roster, a directory or a person's name. A standalone people surface was the obvious alternative and was rejected twice over: a deployment-wide shift board is a whole console surface, and it was justified only by a case — *find John wherever he has wandered* — that disappeared once hailing was confined to roles already permitted on the destination loop. Reading the loop's own permitted roles needs nothing that is not already in the permission grid and the state authority, and it is the **column read** [ADR-0015](./0015-the-admin-console-reads-one-row-at-a-time.md) found legible.

## It looks like a contradiction of ADR-0034, and it is the exception that proves it

[ADR-0034](./0034-the-transmit-bar-is-always-visible-and-the-audience-is-a-count.md) renders the audience as counts and refuses names, for two reasons: names are not actionable, and nobody reads sixteen of them in the second before keying. Both fail here deliberately. In the picker the names **are** actionable — picking one is the entire point — and the operator is not one second from keying, they have opened a picker on purpose.

More exactly: ADR-0034 computes a third bucket — people in reach who have not subscribed — and does not display it, because they are not actionable. **Hailing is the thing that makes that bucket actionable.** So the picker is the one surface where it surfaces, and it surfaces by name for precisely the reason it is hidden everywhere else. This is a consequence of ADR-0034's reasoning rather than a departure from it, and if that reasoning is ever revisited both decisions move together.

[ADR-0033](./0033-the-console-shows-that-someone-is-talking-never-who.md) is untouched. It bans rendering *who is talking*; the picker renders *who is in which seat*, which is roster information the lobby ([ADR-0023](./0023-sign-in-is-to-the-application-and-a-role-is-assumed.md)) already shows. The console still ships no surface on which an operator can read the identity of a talker.

## Consequences

- **Occupancy is not scoped to reach, and loops still are.** Once the picker greys out people it cannot hail, it lists occupants of roles that hold nothing on the destination loop — which is, in effect, everyone on shift, plus the fact that their role cannot hear this loop. [ADR-0019](./0019-presence-is-one-versioned-document-scoped-to-reach.md)'s scoping therefore applies to **loop state**, not to occupancy. This is narrow: a roster leaks no loops and no authority, and who is on shift is the least secret thing in a control room.
- **The picker is a snapshot fetched when opened, not part of the presence document.** Pushing deployment-wide occupancy to every session at the document's tick rate to serve a modal open for seconds is the wrong trade, and keeping it out preserves ADR-0019 exactly. The cost is that a seat vacated between the fetch and the click takes a hail that reaches nobody, and — there being no reply channel ([ADR-0047](./0047-a-hail-is-a-monitoring-directive-without-the-authority.md)) — the hailer is not told. That is the same silent success ADR-0029 accepted for an announcement into a vacant loop.
- **Greyed entries exist to stop an absence lying.** Without them, a person missing from the picker is indistinguishable from a person not on shift, and the operator is left guessing which. This is the *absent-because-denied versus absent-because-nobody-ruled* ambiguity ADR-0015 found in the admin console, arriving through a second door, and it gets the same answer: say which.
- **Filtered is the default because the common case is urgency.** *Get someone here now* should not cost a scan past twenty greyed names; *why can't I get John here?* is a question asked deliberately, and it is what the toggle is for.
- **Off console and `disconnected` are shown against a person, marked.** Both are already tracked and already displayed elsewhere, so neither is new state, and hailing someone who has explicitly stepped away ([ADR-0016](./0016-displayed-state-is-observed-or-asserted.md)) is a wasted action worth knowing about first.
- **A person appears at most once**, because a user occupies exactly one role at a time. Position combining is out of scope, and the flat-by-person view that would have made sense of a user in two seats went with it.
- **This is a third rendering to keep honest.** ADR-0032 warned that every state added to the console costs twice; the picker makes some of it cost three times, and it is the first console surface whose contents are not the loop list.
