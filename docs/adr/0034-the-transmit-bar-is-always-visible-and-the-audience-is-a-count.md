# The transmit bar is always visible, and the audience is a count

Both console views carry the same **transmit bar**: the armed set written out in words, the audience as two numbers, and the key state. It is positioned differently in each view and worded identically in both, and it is never scrolled away. It is the console's answer to *who am I about to talk to*, and that question has to be answerable without moving anything.

The **audience is rendered as counts, not names** — `6 hearing`, `1 present, not hearing`. The third bucket, people in reach who chose not to subscribe, is computed and not shown: they are not actionable, and a loop exists precisely so that an emitter can stop tracking who is on the other end. Sixteen names is also more than anyone reads in the second before keying, and the two numbers that remain are the two that change a decision — the first is reassurance, the second is a warning.

**A zero audience never blocks a transmission.** Emitting to a loop nobody is hearing is legal and always has been ([ADR-0001](./0001-the-loop-is-the-only-destination.md), [ADR-0005](./0005-occupancy-means-listening-not-signed-in.md)); the bar renders `0 hearing` in the warning colour and the operator keys anyway if they mean to. Blocking it, or interposing a dialog, would be the console overruling an operator about their own operation.

## Consequences

- **`present, not hearing` is the console's only warning about mute.** It is the count that says *these people believe they are covering this loop and will not hear you*, and it is the compensating signal for several gaps recorded elsewhere: mute defeating an announcement ([ADR-0029](./0029-an-announcement-is-an-ordinary-transmission.md)), and mute defeating a monitoring directive ([ADR-0035](./0035-a-monitoring-directive-promotes-a-loop-it-does-not-police-it.md)).
- **Presets live on the transmit bar**, beside the key control, in both views. A preset is momentary and replaces only the arm set ([ADR-0013](./0013-arming-is-independent-of-subscription.md)); placing it among the loop cards would make it look like a persistent grouping, which is the profile confusion that ADR-0013 exists to prevent.
- **Blind-armed loops are named in words, not just coloured.** A loop armed but not monitored is legal and is the one state where the operator's mental model and reality come apart, so the card says so rather than relying on a border.
- **A hot latch is announced by a persistent banner with a running age, plus the key control itself rendering live in both views.** [ADR-0022](./0022-latch-is-never-derived-from-a-momentary-press.md) already ruled out sidetone, expiry and an alarm, so the console's entire answer is visual — and a banner alone becomes chrome, which is why the in-context signal is required too. ⚠️ **This holds only while VoxLoop is visible.** v1 has no unfocused push-to-talk, so a latched operator is looking at the console by definition. The Tauri wrapper ([ADR-0020](./0020-the-browser-is-the-client.md)) is what breaks that assumption, by letting someone latch open with telemetry focused and VoxLoop behind it — so the wrapper, not the console, is what makes a non-visual latch indication necessary, and it is not v1 work.
- **Per-loop volume is behind a cog on the card and the row**, opening a modal scoped to that loop, holding only volume in v1. Loudest-wins already resolves overlap in the media plane ([ADR-0006](./0006-mediasoup-carries-the-audio.md)), so per-loop volume is personalisation rather than a live operational control, and it should not be nudgeable from the main surface. A single global settings modal was rejected: it would need a loop picker, rebuilding the list the operator just clicked from.
- **The unshown third bucket surfaces in exactly one place.** People in reach who have not subscribed are computed and hidden here because they are not actionable — and [ADR-0048](./0048-the-hail-picker-is-the-only-place-the-console-names-a-person.md) is where hailing makes them actionable, so the hail picker names them. That is a consequence of the reasoning above rather than an exception to it: if the counts-not-names rule is ever revisited, both decisions move together.
