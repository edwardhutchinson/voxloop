# Priority defeats attenuation and nothing else

A **priority transmission** plays at full gain in every subscriber's client, whatever they have set that loop's volume to. That is the whole of interruptive priority in v1. It attenuates no other talker, it does not defeat mute, and it compels no subscription.

[ADR-0007](./0007-the-client-emits-one-stream.md) chose a per-talker downlink with client-side mixing partly to keep ducking and interruptive priority available, and recorded that whether v1 exercises the option was undecided. This is that decision, and it exercises the narrowest part of it.

**Two failures were on the table and only one of them is ours to fix.** A busy loop drowning an urgent call is real, but it is a *contention* problem among people who can all hear each other: they stop talking, and the shift lead intervenes by voice. [ADR-0014](./0014-authority-acts-on-emission-are-transient.md) already made exactly this retreat for airtime discipline, on the grounds that mission-control practice handles it socially rather than through tooling, and nothing here argues differently.

The other failure has no human in the loop at all. You turned Sim down to 20% three hours ago, something urgent happened on Sim, and nobody in the room knows you cannot hear it. **Per-loop volume is the one attenuation in VoxLoop that nothing warns anybody about.** Mute is surfaced twice over — it drops a loop to `away` and it is counted as `present, not hearing` on the transmit bar ([ADR-0034](./0034-the-transmit-bar-is-always-visible-and-the-audience-is-a-count.md)) — but a loop turned down to 10% is invisible to the operator who turned it down and to the person emitting into it. Priority is scoped to precisely that gap.

**Ducking other talkers is rejected**, though ADR-0007 paid to keep it available. [ADR-0033](./0033-the-console-shows-that-someone-is-talking-never-who.md) means the console shows *that* a loop is active and structurally never *who*, so when your audio drops the console cannot tell you what caused it — and the standing rule is that displayed state is factual. Ducking is also the only mechanism considered here that makes audio the operator explicitly asked for get quieter, which is the console doing something they did not ask for. And it needs an answer to "who gets ducked" spanning loops the priority talker is not on, an authority question the grid cannot express, since `control` is scoped by loop ([ADR-0012](./0012-operational-authority-is-the-control-rung.md)).

**Mute stays sovereign.** Overriding it would make ADR-0034's `present, not hearing` count a lie in exactly the case it exists for, and three other decisions lean on that count — mute defeating an announcement ([ADR-0029](./0029-an-announcement-is-an-ordinary-transmission.md)), mute defeating a monitoring directive ([ADR-0035](./0035-a-monitoring-directive-promotes-a-loop-it-does-not-police-it.md)), and the bar's own warning. It would also deliver audio to someone whose headset is off.

**Reach is untouched too.** Priority governs gain, never who receives. A priority mechanism that also pinned a subscription would be re-litigating ADR-0035's finding that compelled hearing is unavailable at any price, under a new name. There is correspondingly **no personal opt-out** — no "do not override my volume" setting — because that would make the feature nothing, and mute already provides the escape.

**Self-dim on transmit** — your own downlink dropping while you key — is not in v1. It is personalisation rather than priority and has no authority dimension at all; it belongs to [#22](https://github.com/edwardhutchinson/voxloop/issues/22) if it is ever wanted.

## Consequences

- **Per-loop volume is no longer the whole story for gain.** The cog on a card ([ADR-0034](./0034-the-transmit-bar-is-always-visible-and-the-audience-is-a-count.md)) sets a level that somebody else can override for the length of their transmission. This is the first control in the console whose effect is not entirely the operator's own.
- **Priority bypasses loudest-wins rather than competing with it.** ADR-0007 resolves overlapping loop volumes by taking the loudest applicable; a priority transmission simply plays at full, so the rule does not run.
- **Priority applies to the whole arm set**, the way Cut applies to the whole uplink and for the same reason: the client emits one stream and the server fans it out ([ADR-0007](./0007-the-client-emits-one-stream.md)), so priority is an attribute of the transmission, not of a destination. **You cannot be priority on one armed loop and ordinary on another.** A wide arm set therefore overrides volume on many loops at once, which is the abuse vector — see [ADR-0046](./0046-priority-is-keyed-not-held.md) for what watches it.
- **Cut beats priority.** Cut closes the server-side fan-out ([ADR-0014](./0014-authority-acts-on-emission-are-transient.md)) while priority is a gain decision in the receiving client, so there is no contest and no ordering to define. This matters: it means the existing escalation still works against someone abusing priority.
- **The priority attribute rides signalling, not media.** ADR-0007 already established that loop attribution reaches the client through the presence document rather than the media path, and priority is the same kind of fact. So there is a window in which the audio has arrived and the priority attribute has not, and **the first moments of an urgent call can play attenuated**. This is unavoidable while ADR-0007 stands; the alternative would be carrying it in the media plane.
- **VoxLoop cannot guarantee that anyone hears anything, and never will.** It can guarantee only that if a subscriber is subscribed and unmuted, their own attenuation will not hide it. This is the honest limit and should be stated to a pilot customer rather than left implied.
