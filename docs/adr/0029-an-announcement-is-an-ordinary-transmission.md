# An announcement is an ordinary transmission

The brief asks for an API "generic enough that someone can inject programmatically events and stuff like that". VoxLoop's answer is that there is nothing special about injected voice at all: **a service principal is a talker.** Text arrives over HTTP on the token-bearing path of [ADR-0026](./0026-one-credential-and-the-media-path-carries-none.md), the server synthesises it on the box, and the audio enters the media plane as a producer — where [ADR-0007](./0007-the-client-emits-one-stream.md)'s fan-out carries it to the loops the bound role may emit on, exactly as it carries a human keying up.

Giving injected audio a delivery path of its own was the obvious alternative and it buys nothing. Everything an announcement needs already exists and is had for free: the recording tap of [ADR-0009](./0009-recording-taps-plain-rtp-on-loopback.md) captures it per (talker, loop) with no new work, attribution is the bound role per [ADR-0027](./0027-a-service-principal-acts-through-a-role.md), loudest-wins volume applies, and the audience computation of [ADR-0019](./0019-presence-is-one-versioned-document-scoped-to-reach.md) already knows who will hear it. A separate path would have needed its own routing, its own permission story and its own place in the recording seam — and a permission story outside the grid is the second authority layer [ADR-0011](./0011-a-permission-is-one-cell-on-the-grid.md) exists to forbid.

**A service principal never carries media over the network.** It has no WebRTC transport, no ICE, no DTLS and no client. It posts text; the audio is created inside the server and injected directly. This is the strongest form of ADR-0026's rule that the media path carries no credential — for a service principal there is no media path to reach at all.

## The forceful second endpoint was designed, and then dropped

For most of [#11](https://github.com/edwardhutchinson/voxloop/issues/11) the design had **two** endpoints: one that behaved like a talker, and one that reached people *regardless of their own settings* — bypassing mute, per-loop volume and even non-subscription, while never bypassing the grid. That distinction is worth recording because it is a reasonable design and it will be proposed again.

It died on the question of what confers it. Forcing audio into somebody's ears against their own settings is operational authority by any reading, so the gate had to be the `control` rung of [ADR-0012](./0012-operational-authority-is-the-control-rung.md). Once gated that way it stopped being a convenience endpoint and became **a second mechanism for compelling people, sitting beside monitoring directives** ([ADR-0004](./0004-monitoring-directives-are-enforced-and-additive.md)), which already compel monitoring, are already gated on `control`, and are already enforced, additive-only and visible until cleared. The two differed mainly in duration. Carrying both into a control room, where an operator must know at a glance why their audio changed, was not worth what the second one bought.

So there is one endpoint, and **`control` plays no part in announcements at all**. The ticket asked whether an injected announcement should respect a user's listen permissions or override them; the answer is that it respects them completely, and it respects their personalisation too, because there is no override mechanism anywhere to reach for.

**The accepted consequence: v1 cannot guarantee that an announcement is heard.** A user who mutes `Ground Alarms` will not hear `Ground Alarms`, and nothing in the system overrides that. Two things already in the model soften it more than it first appears. Muting a loop you staff drops it to `away` ([ADR-0013](./0013-arming-is-independent-of-subscription.md)), so the deafness is visible to everyone else rather than silent. And mute is not an unsubscribe, so talking indicators keep arriving — a muted loop still shows that something is being said on it. Whether the gap needs filling at all is deliberately left open.

## The call

**Text plus an explicit list of loops.** No wildcard, no "all", and an empty list is a refusal rather than a default. Reach is capped by the grid — the bound role must hold `emit` on every loop named — but a wildcard would still turn one typo into every loop that role can reach, and reach caps blast radius without excusing it.

**A loop the role cannot reach refuses the whole call**, rather than delivering to the permitted subset. This is deliberately the opposite of the preset rule in ADR-0013, and the difference is the reason: a preset is shared across occupants of differing reach, so silent narrowing is the only way it can work at all, whereas a service principal has exactly one bound role and a loop it cannot reach is a configuration error. Partial delivery would let a misconfigured script appear to work for months.

**Accepted or refused synchronously, and nothing after that.** No delivery status, no callback, no outcome record. The accepted consequence is that **an announcement into a vacant or wholly-muted loop succeeds and nobody hears it, and the caller never learns** — the same class of deliberate gap as the open mic in [ADR-0022](./0022-latch-is-never-derived-from-a-momentary-press.md).

**A service principal is one talker with one uplink, so its announcements serialise.** It cannot key twice any more than a person can. Announcements from *different* service principals may overlap, exactly as two humans may. Serialisation is free and needs no queueing policy beyond one rule: **an announcement is refused when the existing backlog would delay it past roughly thirty seconds.** A per-request validity deadline was rejected as caller-facing surface for a problem the server can see better; one number in configuration bounds both memory and staleness, and "event in thirty seconds" arriving twenty minutes late is worse than never arriving in a voice operators are supposed to trust. A **maximum length** is capped for the same reason.

**The source name is composed by the server**, from the bound role, and prepended to the text — `"Ground Alarms: evacuate"`. The caller supplies the body only and can neither set nor suppress the prefix, because a caller that could set it could announce itself as `Flight Director` and ADR-0027's attribution guarantee would be worth nothing.

**The console marks an announcement explicitly.** The presence document carries a flag distinguishing a synthesised transmission from a human talking, rather than leaving it to be derived from "roles no human is eligible for" — derivation by convention is exactly the sort of thing that quietly breaks. Given that a muted loop cannot be heard, the talking indicator is the only way an operator learns an announcement happened at all.

**Announcements are additive and duck nothing.** They play on top of whatever is already on the loop.

## Consequences

- **[#19](https://github.com/edwardhutchinson/voxloop/issues/19) inherits the whole audibility question, not merely an example.** With no override endpoint, ducking is the only remaining mechanism by which an urgent announcement could cut through a busy loop. If interruptive priority also lands outside v1, then v1's answer to *"how do I make sure everyone hears this?"* is **"key up and say it yourself"** — which is a defensible answer for a voice system, but it should be given deliberately rather than discovered.
- **Announcements are not audited.** An announcement is a transmission, and [ADR-0028](./0028-the-audit-log-records-decisions-not-traffic.md) puts traffic in recording's hands rather than the audit log's. The forceful endpoint would have changed this — compelling people is an authority act — and its removal takes the exception with it. Issuing and revoking a service token remains audited, as ADR-0027 already requires.
- **A service principal still never staffs a loop.** Nothing here disturbs ADR-0027: announcements confer reach, never occupancy, and a loop is not `staffed` because something can speak on it.
- **Two service principals can talk over each other**, and neither will know. This is the same situation as two humans keying at once, and it is left alone for the same reason.
