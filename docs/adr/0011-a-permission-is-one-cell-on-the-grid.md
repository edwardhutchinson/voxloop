# A permission is one cell on the role × loop grid, and nothing else

Voice authority is a single value held by a **(role, loop)** pair, one of an ordered four: `none`, `monitor`, `emit`, `control`, each rung carrying everything below it. There is no second layer. No per-user grants, no per-user denies, no explicit deny that beats a grant, no precedence rules — evaluating a permission is one lookup, and an absent cell means `none` means denied.

The brief asked for "a vector of their permissions ... which you can overlap to see who can speak to and who can listen to what", and flagged that it would be "tricky to easily understand". This ADR is the answer to that flag. The whole model is one grid of roughly 15 roles against 20 loops, so **"who can hear loop X" is column X, and "what can this person reach" is their role's row** — both answerable by reading, with nothing to cross-reference and nowhere for an exception to hide.

**Listen and emit are not independent axes.** A ladder was chosen over two bits because emit-without-monitor is a hazard rather than a feature: an operator armed on a loop they may not hear cannot tell they are talking over someone and cannot hear the reply. The one genuine emit-without-monitor case — the event-injection and TTS service ([#11](https://github.com/edwardhutchinson/voxloop/issues/11)) — is a service principal, not a role, and is modelled separately. openvocs independently arrived at the same ordering with `none | recv | send` ([#15](https://github.com/edwardhutchinson/voxloop/issues/15)); the fourth rung is ours, and is [ADR-0012](./0012-operational-authority-is-the-control-rung.md).

Note this ladder is *permission*, not subscription. A user may hold `monitor` on a loop and choose not to subscribe, and may emit to a loop they are not subscribed to — see [ADR-0013](./0013-arming-is-independent-of-subscription.md).

**This retires the user-scoped override accepted by [ADR-0002](./0002-permissions-attach-to-role-and-loop.md).** That override was kept "sparingly" as the escape hatch for one-off cases, and three of them were expected to need it. Each turned out to have a better home: a mid-event silencing became **Cut**, a transient act in the media plane rather than a permission ([ADR-0014](./0014-authority-acts-on-emission-are-transient.md)); listen-only became the seeded `Observer` role rather than a user flag; and the brief's request-access flow became an **access request** that system administration resolves by *editing configuration* — a grid cell, or an eligibility grant — with the request attached as the reason. With no use case left, the override was costing the model its single best property for nothing: any per-user exception layer means column X is never the whole answer, and an administrator must scan ~190 users before trusting it.

## Consequences

- **Granting one person one extra loop always costs a role.** There is no quick per-person patch and there never will be. Roles are cheap to create and stable across staff turnover — [ADR-0002](./0002-permissions-attach-to-role-and-loop.md)'s own argument — but this is a real ergonomic cost, taken deliberately in exchange for a grid that can be trusted at a glance.
- **The brief's per-user permission vector is fully rejected**, not merely reshaped.
- **A permission change applies to live sessions immediately.** Revoking `emit` cuts a transmission mid-word ([ADR-0008](./0008-emission-is-armed-by-the-server-and-keyed-by-the-client.md)); revoking `monitor` drops the subscription symmetrically, and tells the user why. A monitoring directive ([ADR-0004](./0004-monitoring-directives-are-enforced-and-additive.md)) that targets a role which subsequently loses `monitor` on a directed loop stops applying to it — the directive is not a grant and cannot outrank the grid.
- **Terminology**: [ADR-0002](./0002-permissions-attach-to-role-and-loop.md), [ADR-0005](./0005-occupancy-means-listening-not-signed-in.md) and [ADR-0008](./0008-emission-is-armed-by-the-server-and-keyed-by-the-client.md) were written before the ladder was named and say `recv`/`send`. Read those as `monitor`/`emit`; the canonical names are in `CONTEXT.md`.
- **The admin console must render the grid well enough that reading it is genuinely how administrators reason.** The model has been shaped for that and can go no further alone. ⚠️ **Amended by [ADR-0015](./0015-the-admin-console-reads-one-row-at-a-time.md).** A prototype ([#20](https://github.com/edwardhutchinson/voxloop/issues/20)) found the *matrix* is not how they reason — it is read a row at a time, as a list. The single-lookup model is unaffected, and the two reads above hold; only the rendering changed.
