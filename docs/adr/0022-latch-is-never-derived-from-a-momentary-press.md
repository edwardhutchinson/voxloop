# Latch is never derived from a momentary press

Latched emission always has its **own binding**. It is never produced by how a momentary control was pressed — not by a short tap, not by a double tap, not by a held duration. The v1 defaults are **`` ` `` for momentary and `` Shift+` `` for latch**, both user-configurable.

The tempting alternative is one control carrying both: hold to talk, tap to latch. It was rejected because **it makes an open mic the failure mode of a hardware fault.** A button that stops reporting release, or a dropped report from a source that has gone away, is indistinguishable from a deliberate short tap — so the exact malfunction [ADR-0021](./0021-ptt-input-is-a-level-with-liveness.md)'s liveness flag exists to catch would silently latch the operator open instead. Separate bindings keep the mental model flat: **the control in your hand always means "I am talking while I hold this"**, on every device, in every tier.

## Consequences

- **A single-button device is momentary-only.** There is no gesture that reaches latch, so latch is available from the keyboard and from the console, and a headset inline button or a footswitch can only ever be momentary. This is a deliberate loss of capability in exchange for the guarantee above.
- **Latch ships without further mitigation in v1.** Periodic sidetone in the latcher's own ears, a hard expiry, and an unmissable console state were all considered and dropped. The judgement recorded here is that a latched open mic is **a UX affordance, not a safety-critical failure** — no worse than an open microphone in a video call. The operator owns their microphone, the console shows the state, and two mechanisms already stand: [ADR-0018](./0018-no-signalling-channel-means-no-emission-path.md) drops a latch after roughly two seconds of `unconfirmed`, and [ADR-0014](./0014-authority-acts-on-emission-are-transient.md)'s **Cut** lets operational authority stop it. A future reader should read this as a considered scope decision rather than an oversight.
- **The default keys are safe rather than conventional.** Space is the usual push-to-talk key and was rejected: it activates whatever control has focus, it scrolls, and it types — and VoxLoop's console is full of focusable arm buttons and loop tiles. `` ` `` has no browser binding, activates no control, and conflicts only while typing. `CapsLock` was rejected outright because its `keyup` is unreliable on macOS, and **an unreliable release on a PTT key is an open mic**.
- **PTT keys are live only when a talk-capable role is signed in**, and never while focus is in a text field or on an interactive control. The console must not place a focusable control where an operator's hands rest.
- **The wrapper's hotkey cannot use the same default.** A *global* `` ` `` would take the backtick from every application on the machine, so [ADR-0020](./0020-the-browser-is-the-client.md)'s hotkey defaults to a modified combination. The operator's PTT key therefore differs between the browser and the wrapper unless they converge the two themselves — which per [ADR-0021](./0021-ptt-input-is-a-level-with-liveness.md) they can, because both bindings are theirs.
