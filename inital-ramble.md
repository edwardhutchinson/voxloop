# High-Level Idea & Use Case

The idea behind Foxloop comes from the line of work that I've been involved in. So I work in spacecraft operations, and part of that in Europe at least at ESA, European Space Agency, they have this concept of a voice loop. The voice loop is effectively a physical console that lives next to each operator's workstation, so when they're controlling a satellite, and with that, they can speak to relevant parties, so they might engage the microphone, then speak to the ground station, or speak to another colleague. But a big part of what that product is, is you can subscribe to different quote-unquote loops. So a loop might be use would subscribe to listen to the comms coming out of a particular ground station, or you would subscribe to comms coming out of a certain entity. Now, traditionally, those entities aren't people, they are an instance like a ground station or a support engineer, and not necessarily the engineer themselves. That's the traditional case. What we want to do with VoxLoop, this application, is have a purely software-based implementation. My current company doesn't have a solution for the voice loop, so this is what this application is intending to solve, but I don't want to limit the remit just to spacecraft operations. I think this can serve a broader domain than spacecraft operations because essentially all it is is a collection of people. Those people can be combined into groups. People can then subscribe to listen to those groups and or subscribe to speak to those groups.

# Administration

The key part of what the application is, is that an administrator has overall right to control the permissions of who can listen to certain entities and who entities can speak to on the user side, the user should effectively have what I'm imagining right now is a grid where they can select to subscribe to different entities and they would then receive all of the comms of those entities. Now of course more than one entity that you're subscribed to can speak at once and that's fine. That's how this application works. You're subscribing to all of the audio. You might want to tweak the volume levels of each different entity so that you have more perhaps there's a manager loop that you're subscribed to and that would have full volume and then it might have a reduced loop for those entities that you're interested in but perhaps aren't as important as what the overall manager is saying. There should also be some mechanism where when a user pushes to talk to emit their voice and their voice stream to the entities they're choosing to they can do that to all of the entities that they're choosing to admit their voice to, or there should be a separate user, easy user way of just emitting their voice perhaps to specific users in certain circumstances. What I also want from a user and is a common thing that happens is that entities might be interacting across a bigger group loop, so there's loads of people listening at first, and the action from that is: okay, actually, we need these two people in specific to speak together. Not everyone needs to hear about that, so they should quite easily be able to go away and just create a direct isolated comms channel just between the entities that are relevant. Of course there are certain things we need to navigate with that in that if someone is on an isolated stream and they're requested back on a certain loop, we need to be able to have notifications to request them to join, or indeed admins or elevated permission people may just be able to override and inject their voice into a certain stream.

# Loops

A loop is an entity that can emit their voice and or that another entity can choose to subscribe to and listen to that entity might be an individual user, but a loop, perhaps we need a different term for this, but a loop could be a set of users that you've brought in together. So the example I can think from the top of my head is in the spacecraft operations world, you might have a bunch of flight dynamics engineers. Those in isolation could each be their own entity, but collectively they could be a loop. So you might subscribe to the flight dynamics loop and you would get all of the output from those people in that loop providing the permissions are set up to do that way or when you choose to speak to the flight dynamics loop or speak and you're outputting to the flight dynamics loop all of those engineers that are a part of that loop will get that information. We do need to think about the terminology between a loop that is one person and a loop that is a group of people.

It's one to discuss but perhaps a user is only ever a user, and they can assign themselves effectively to a loop or a set of loops. And those loops can be set up so that they can be only allow one person ever to be take the role of that loop. So for example, it could be an operator may only ever allow one entity to be its loop and we should be able to see who is currently taking on the role as that loop in this case. Or it could be that we have a set of support engineers which can hold any number of people, and then those people are currently actively a part of that group so that when you omit to your voice to that group, it reaches a set of engineers. Any one of those could respond, for example. We would need to be able to see in UI in these cases when there are no entities currently taking the role or the mantle of that loop. For example, if you're speaking to the support engineers loop, you need to know whether anyone's actually behind it or not. So perhaps it goes auto-offline if no one's currently taking on the role.

# Events

Something I would like to introduce is these notions of events and or notifications. The idea being that someone could set up scheduled notifications that are emitted to certain people or everyone. So, for example, it may be that we're expecting certain people to be aware of something that's happening at this particular time, and an administrator or someone with the relevant permissions could then set up the schedule event for us to emit an event message saying this event is happening in X seconds. So, this will be some text that we need to output as SAM, so a text-to-voice type system.

We need this API to be pretty generic, so if for whatever reason someone wanted to be notified when a certain file was available, there should be a way for an administrator to configure watching for that file, or not watching for the file, but configure an API injection of voice to text based on whatever condition they're listening for. We don't have to implement that condition, but we just need the API to be generic enough that someone can inject programmatically events and stuff that such.

# Actions
Alongside the voice interface, there should be some simple actions that a user can also do. They should be able to, for example, say when they are off console, i.e. they're no longer monitoring the application, I don't know if they've gone on their tea break or gone to the bathroom, something like that. We don't need to know what they're doing, obviously, but we just need to wait for them to say that they're not currently listening. Users should be able to ping someone to join a certain loop or ping someone to start listening to their loop. So if they notice, for example, the support engineers aren't currently listening to them, they should be able to ping a notification over to those support engineers to join such that they are listening to this certain person.


# Permissions

In terms of permissions, we've touched on this already, but it should be two levels. Oh, well, one level of permissions from the administrator. The administrator, or someone with elevated permissions for the group of people they're responsible for, should be able to control who can output to what and who can listen to what. So, effectively, for each user, you have like a vector of their permissions and which you can overlap to see who can speak to and who can listen to what. This should be configurable by the admin, and perhaps from a user's perspective, they should be able to go in and request access to listen to a certain person, and then the admin can respond to that, By approving or denying.

An administrator should always at any point in time be able to control whether someone can emit or not. For example, if something critical is going on and a certain person is taking up a lot of the airtime without merit on a certain loop, an administrator should be able to come in and just kick them off that loop or prevent them from admitting to certain people and so on and so forth. It's going to be tricky to easily understand how those permission vectors align and stuff. So yeah, we'll have to think about this.

A final thing is that this application, specifically VoxLoop, will likely have to support permissions on an entity level as well. So, for example, it might be that an engineer is part of the support engineers group, but there may be certain streams that they're not actually allowed to listen to at all, even though the loop itself, even though they can partake in the loop itself, there might be certain sensitive data on certain other loops that they're not allowed to listen to. I think this is one we can put on the back burner for now, but it's something to be aware of.

# Interface

There's scope for this to be deployed in different ways. So, for example, it could be an application on an iOS or Android device. I think initially, what we're looking at is a web-based application, and from here we should be able to manage everything. So, that is the admins should have their own console to do all of that stuff that we've discussed. And then, users, it should be a really simple interface so that they can subscribe to and emit to people quite easily. It should be a pretty professional-looking UI, quite minimal, not bright colours, quite muted. But the key thing here is the users should be presented with a very easy way of understanding who they're going to admit to and who they're currently listening to. They should be able to see when people are actively speaking. They should be able to control the volume levels of the various people that they are subscribed to, and they should have pretty simple ways of accessing the application.

Users also need to be able to easily see who they are admitting to and who is listening to them, as we spoke about in the actions section. This has got to be super simple to see so that they can very quickly and easily request someone actually listen to them if they think they should be.

# Default Configurations

Users to some extent should be able to control that default configuration, so they might by default listen to a certain user on a higher volume than everyone else. They might also subscribe to certain loops, which should all be configurable from a user perspective. From an administrator perspective, the administrator should be able to enforce that this user must listen to this loop, and this user must listen to this loop, or this user must emit to this loop. Perhaps we can have different profiles that people can switch between, and it could be that an administrator enforces at a certain time, these profiles have to be adhered to as a minimum. Of course, a user can add to that so they can subscribe to more than what the minimum default is.

# Deployment

In theory there are different ways in which we can offer this application to people. The use case that I have now is for a company that wouldn't want to subscribe to a cloud or software as a service cloud based approach, they would want to control and make sure all of their data stays within the company. So in this case, here we would have to offer the application as something that they can deploy themselves. I'm not sure if that would be the case for all of the different potential customers that we might have, but we have to work to a use case right now, so we need to think about how we would build the application in a way that could support that. I'm flexible on the tech stack that we use, especially on the back end. I think we want to find the balance between the best stack for our long term development, but also velocity initially. I think we're obviously going to have to have a bit of a focus on performance here because we're going to be streaming quite a lot potentially. I think it will be more spike load than continuous in the use case that I have in my mind now. In particular, there would be very small windows where there would be lots of people on the loops and listening and extended periods where very few people are monitoring loops. In terms of the front end, again, I'm quite flexible. I do prefer Svelt if that makes sense, just because it seems to be much more efficient and I think we need to be confident that state is accurately represented. People need to always be aware of who they're speaking to and who they're listening to. We don't really have much room for the UI presenting something that's not factual.

For our initial use case we can test this out with a small number of users. So it might be that we deploy this sort of pseudo-manually initially on over VPS, for example, that we could use but I'm keen to understand whether that is actually the best thing to do initially or whether we should work towards actually how we're going to deploy this properly.

# Longer Term

Some things I do want to implement longer term are monitoring these voice streams. So it could be that people want to record voice streams so they can look back at certain events that happened and what the communication was like. They might use that for training purposes. They might use that just to support their investigations. But a bigger thing alongside this is it would be nice if we could process those voice streams to look out for certain events. So if someone ever stated event X is happening on MAC, we could pass that and then have a notification or add to a notification stream that Event X supposedly happened at Mark, for example, and all things like this. So we might someone might want to configure it so that whenever they say log, it would pass the text after that word log and put that into a database somewhere so that they can retrieve it later. There's all sort of these AI style workflows that I think we can introduce longer term, but they shouldn't be the focus initially. It's just something to bear in mind when we're scoping the infrastructure and initially building this application.
